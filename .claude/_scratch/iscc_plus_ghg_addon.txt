1.      Introduction

The purpose of the document “ISCC PLUS GHG Add-on Document” is to
explain the use of the voluntary GHG add-on under ISCC PLUS for stating
greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions values along the supply chain and to
provide the methodology, rules and guidelines for calculating and verifying
GHG emissions and emissions reduction. Within ISCC PLUS, the verification
of GHG emissions can be added by applying the 205-01 “GHG Emissions”
add-on.

The ISCC requirements regarding GHG emissions apply to all relevant supply
chain elements from raw material production to the distribution of the final
product, including cultivation or extraction, all processing steps, and the
transport and distribution of intermediate and final products.

The system users can follow two different methodologies under ISCC PLUS
GHG add-on. The two different methodologies that can be applied are:

     1. Methodology introduced in the System Document ISCC EU 205 –
        Greenhouse Gas Emissions

     2. Methodology introduced in the System Document ISCC EU 205-1 –
        Renewable Fuels of Non-Biological Origin (RFNBO) and Recycled
        Carbon Fuels (RCF) Greenhouse Gas Emissions

If the add-on is applied, the System Documents (ISCC EU 205 or ISCC EU
205-1) apply equally for ISCC EU and ISCC PLUS, with some differing
requirements under ISCC PLUS which are described in the following sub-
chapters.

Under ISCC PLUS, it is possible to verify the calculated GHG emissions based
on the methodologies described in this document, ISCC EU 205 and ISCC EU
205-1 documents. Any claims regarding GHG Emission reductions based on
the calculations on the above-mentioned methodologies are beyond the scope
of ISCC PLUS GHG Add-on.

In any case the GHG add-on must be implemented in the entire supply chain
up to the entity claiming a value on the outgoing product.

It should be noted that GHG Add-on cannot be applied for Mass Balancing
options Free Attribution Approach and Fuel Use Excluded Attribution
Approach under Credit Method. As an exception for Free attribution approach,
GHG Add-on can only be applied when complete reflection of input
characteristics is reflected. When compensation of input characteristics is
applied (either by system user or on received certified input materials), GHG
add-on cannot be applied. For more information on reflection of input
characteristics, please refer to System Document 203-2 Chain of Custody.

2.      GHG Add-on following the ISCC EU 205 –
        Greenhouse Gas Emissions


ISCC PLUS system users can freely choose to apply the voluntary GHG add-
on. When choosing to disclose the GHG emissions information from the
relevant supply chain and production of product, the total GHG emissions
value must be made available. Please refer to System Document ISCC EU –
205 Greenhouse Gas Emissions for further information regarding
requirements for an individual GHG emissions calculation and provision of
GHG information. The total GHG emissions value should be displayed in terms
of either kg CO2eq/dry-ton (for raw materials and intermediate products) or g
CO2eq/MJ (for final fuels).

System users have three different options for providing GHG emissions values
under the ISCC PLUS GHG add-on as listed below:

     1. Total Default Values (TDV)

     2. Actual Values (individually calculated values)

     3. Disaggregated Default Values (DDV) which allows a combination of
        default values and actual values.

Apart from abovementioned elements for the calculation of total GHG
emissions value, RED III also provides the option for a GHG bonus (eb) if the
degraded land (including land that was formerly in agricultural use) is restored
and if the biomass is obtained from the restored degraded land. When the land
is either significantly salinated or presented significantly low organic matter
content and has been severely eroded, is considered severely degraded. The
bonus is thus applicable in case of conversion of the land to agricultural use
that increases carbon stocks. The bonus can only be applied and attributed if
the evidence is provided (refer to the System Document ISCC EU 205 –
Greenhouse Gas Emissions).

TDVs and DDVs are provided in RED III in Annex V and Annex VI. DDVs are
available for cultivation (eec), processing (ep), and transport & distribution (etd). Using
these values allows combining DDVs with actual data from individual GHG
calculations.

In case TDVs in accordance with RED III have been used for demonstrating the
GHG impact of a product, no actual figures for the GHG values can be reported.
However, it must be indicated on the Sustainability Declaration (SD): “Total default
values according to RED III applied” .

Similarly, when using DDVs for one or more elements of the calculation
formula, this information must be indicated on the outgoing SD as well:
“Disaggregated default values according to RED III applied”. Please refer
to the System Document ISCC EU 205 – Greenhouse Gas Emissions for
detailed restrictions on the use of default values.

Actual values are calculated based on RED III methodology and must always
be conducted at the point in the supply chain where they originate. It is not
possible to calculate actual values retrospectively for elements of the
upstream supply chain. An individual GHG calculation for raw materials and
intermediate products must always be indicated in terms of kg CO2eq/dry-ton.
Mode of transport and transport distance (in km) should also be provided by
system users in case default values are not considered for the GHG emissions
calculation. Values from the specific “NUTS2 region” report can be used as an
alternative to DDVs or actual values. In case NUTS2 values are used, system
users should provide this information in the SD Annex, in the “product(s)
related information” field.

2.1     Specific Requirements for Elements of the Supply Chain
If the system user chooses to apply the voluntary ISCC PLUS GHG add-on,
there are specific requirements depending on the scope of certificate. In this
subchapter, the responsibilities for each element of the supply chain are
described.

2.1.1     Farms or Plantations/Forest Sourcing Areas
When applicable, farms or plantations are responsible to provide emissions
values for extraction and cultivation (eec). In case el, esca and eb are applied,

2.1.2      First Gathering Point

The first gathering point is responsible for determining and documenting GHG

for verifying whether specific options to state greenhouse gas emissions (for

applied.

2.1.3      Point of Origin for Waste and Residues

At the point of origin, GHG emissions of waste and residues are considered to
be zero for the waste generation. Further emissions associated to waste
transportation and treatment must be considered.

2.1.5     Paper Trader and Trader with Storage

Paper traders do not calculate emissions from transport but only forward
information on outgoing sustainability declarations as received. Trader with
storage do not calculate emissions, however, only provide information for
transport distance(s) and transport type(s) towards their storage site on the
outgoing sustainability declaration. The next element in the supply chain (e.g.
processing unit) after the trader with storage bears the responsibility for
emissions calculation from upstream transport activities.

2.2     Deviations with respect to Emission Factors

Within ISCC PLUS, emission factors (EF) of input materials can be individually
calculated or come from official sources like the Annex IX of the IR 2022/996,
Renewable Energy Directive (RED) or Annex I of the System Document ISCC
EU – 205 Greenhouse Gas Emissions. Furthermore, values based on LCA
databases such as ECOINVENT or official statistical databases from
government bodies or scientifically peer-reviewed literature can be used, if
applicable. All data gathered from databases or literature shall be based on
the most recent available versions and shall also be updated over time.
Recognized emissions factor sources are:

      > RED III

      > IR 2022/996

      > ISO 14040/44 or ISO 14067.

The methodology used for individual calculations by the supplier must always
be verified according to the ISO standard or alternatively the supplier must be
ISCC/ISO certified so that relevant requirements can be verified during an
ISCC audit.

2.3     Calculation of Regional GHG Values for Cultivation (eec)
If NUTS2 values are not available in non-EU country reports or if DDVs for
cultivation emissions are missing in the RED, ISCC PLUS permits third parties
(e.g. companies, plantation owners, associations) to calculate averages based
on local farming practices, for example, average emissions calculated for
geographical areas at national level or data from a group of farms. The
methodology shall follow ISCC’s requirements and ISCC should be informed
whenever such values are calculated. However, a submission of the GHG
emissions values to the European Commission is not required.

2.4   Calculation of Individual GHG Values for Cultivation (eec)
In case of an individual GHG emissions calculation for a group of farms or
plantations, the averaging of input values and GHG emissions values is
accepted under ISCC PLUS.

2.5   Aggregation of Different GHG Values
It is also possible to use the highest GHG values of the least performing batch
for the same kind of certified input material (material with identical
sustainability characteristic), alternatively to using single values for each
certified input material.

2.6   Allocation of GHG Emissions

Under ISCC PLUS, the allocation of emissions to main and co-products can
be based on the energy content of both products (refer to the System
Document ISCC EU – 205 Greenhouse Gas Emissions), but other types of
allocation (e.g. based on mass) are also possible. The most suitable allocation
method should always be used, e.g. if the main product is used energetically,
an energetic allocation should be applied.

2.7   Life Cycle Coverage
Under ISCC PLUS, the GHG emissions calculation can either cover the whole
life cycle of the product (cradle-to-grave), or only the emissions up to the
factory gate (cradle-to-gate).

It must always be clearly highlighted on the Sustainability Declaration of the
product if the cradle-to-grave or cradle-to-gate approach is used. If required,
further information on the additional emissions to be included for the product
must be provided to the recipient of the material.

2.8   Waste and Residues
Waste and residues shall be considered to have zero GHG emissions up to
the point of their collection, where the waste or residue initially occurs/is
generated (point of origin scope under ISCC PLUS). This is regardless of
whether the materials are further processed into intermediate products before
being processed into the final product.

For example, in the case of UCO, the point of origin would be the restaurants
or plants producing fried products. In the case of rendered animal fats, the
point of origin would be the rendering plant producing animal fat from animal
by-products. If UCO or other wastes originate from households, the point of
origin could be a private company or a municipality responsible for waste
collection.

2.9      Emission Factors for Electricity
In case electricity is supplied by the grid, the emission factors of the
national/country electricity mix shall be used6. The only exceptions to use an
updated emission factor for electricity is:

Option 1 – System user receives renewable electricity via contracts

If the system user has Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) combined with
Energy Attribute Certificates (e.g., Guarantees of Origin (GoO) in the EU or
RECs in the U.S., I-RECs) or comparable proof of no multiple counting, the
system user can choose to apply the supplier specific EF. The following
information shall be a part of the documentation for using supplier specific EF:

       > The energy source from which the energy carrier was produced and
           the start and end dates of production.

       > The type of energy or energy carrier (e.g., electricity, gas–including H2,
           heat, or cooling).

       > The identity, location, type and capacity of the installation where the
           energy or energy carrier was produced.

       > The date on which the installation became operational (i.e., starting
           point of first electricity supply into grid).

       > The date and country of issue and a unique identification number of
           the EAC. (In other words, a proof of no double counting is required).

       > The proof of renewability must ensure that double counting of the
           renewable electricity is avoided. A respective proof of the unit using the
           renewable energy must be provided during the audit.

If those requirements are not fulfilled, or, in case the supplier specific EF is not
available, or contracts are sold to a third party, the emission factors of the
national/country electricity mix shall be used.

Option 2 – System user has a direct connection with the respective unit
producing renewable electricity

In the case that an electricity production plant is connected to the grid, using
the average emission value for electricity from that individual electricity
production plant in the production process is permitted. It must be guaranteed
that there is a direct connection between the production facility and the
individual electricity production plant and it must be possible to validate the
amount of electricity used with a suitable meter.

3.1     Emissions from elastic inputs (ei elastic)
Elastic inputs are those, where the supply can be increased to meet the extra
demand. H2, CO2, natural gas and electricity can be considered as elastic
inputs. Emissions from elastic inputs that are obtained in an incorporated
process shall be calculated based on data of their actual production.
Emissions arising from the input production over the whole supply chain
including extraction of primary energy required to make the input, processing
and transport emissions are counted.

For post-industrial and atmospheric CO2, the supplier of CO2 (issuer of the
sustainability declaration) only provides the effort for the capture and (if
applicable) liquefaction of CO2. The supplier of CO2 does not calculate GHG
emissions. However, the recipient of the CO2 bears the responsibility to
calculate GHG emissions based on the information provided for capture,
liquefaction and transport.

When elastic inputs are not obtained from an incorporated process, emission
factors can be used from them database such as ECOINVENT, IPCC, IEA,
governmental bodies or peer-reviewed publications can be used.

for this approach:

         or is connected to the grid, but evidence can be provided that the
         electricity concerned has been supplied without taking electricity from

3.2     Emissions from rigid inputs (ei rigid)
Rigid inputs are classified as those supply that cannot be expanded to meet
the demands. Rigid inputs include liquid or solid waste streams of non-
renewable origin which are not suitable for material recovery or waste
processing gas and exhaust gas of non-renewable origin. According to EU
2023/1185, all inputs qualifying as a carbon source for the production of
recycled carbon fuels are rigid, as well as outputs produced in fixed ratio by
an incorporated process8 and which represent less than 10 % of the economic

The calculation emissions from rigid inputs shall include the emissions from

3.3   Emissions from existing use or fate (eex-use)
These inputs include avoided emissions such as CO2 equivalent emissions
that would have been emitted if the carbon incorporated in the chemical
composition of the fuel would not have been used for fuel production. Inputs
such as electricity, heat and consumable materials used in the process of
capturing, transporting and storing CO2 should be accounted in the calculation.

In case CO is used as an input, it should be considered any other input that
has an energy content.

3.4   Emissions from Processing (ep)

It should include all direct atmospheric emissions originating from the
processing including combustion, waste treatment and leakages. Hydrogen
leakage is considered as an energy loss, with proportional increase of the
emissions intensity. The use of processing inputs resulting in direct
atmospheric emissions should be accounted for under emissions from
processing.

3.5   Emissions from Transport and Distribution (etd)
This includes the emissions from storage and transport of the final fuels.
Storage and transport related emissions of inputs shall be included in the
emissions attributed to the inputs (ei).

3.6   Emissions from Fuel in Use (eu)
Emissions from the fuel in use refers to the total combustion emissions of the
fuel in use. The greenhouse gases to be considered are CO2, N2O and CH4.

If this approach is followed, this must be made clear for the downstream supply
chain with an additional statement in the ISCC PLUS sustainability declaration,
in the field “product(s) related information” in annex:

“This GHG value, calculated based on the ISCC EU 205-1 methodology, does
not account for the combustion emissions (eu) as the material is not designated
to be used as fuel. If this product is to be used by the recipient for energetic

3.7   Emissions saving from carbon capture and geological storage
      (eccs)

If carbon emissions from the RFNBOs fuel production are permanently stored,
these emissions can be considered as eccs. The requirements for the
geological storage of CO2 should be in accordance with or equivalent to the
Directive 2009/31/EC. However, emissions from storage and transport of CO2
must be considered under ep.

3.8   Life Cycle Coverage
Under ISCC PLUS, the GHG emissions calculation can either cover the whole
life cycle of the product (cradle-to-grave), or only the emissions up to the
factory gate (cradle-to-gate).

It must always be clearly highlighted on the Sustainability Declaration of the
product if cradle-to-grave or cradle-to-gate approach is used. If required,
further information on the additional emissions to be included for the product
must be provided to the recipient of the material.

If cradle-to-gate approach is applied for the product, emissions from fuel in use
(eu) need not be considered for the emissions calculation. However, it should
be noted that emissions for existing use or fate (eex-use) cannot be applied when
omitting eu from calculation. Hence, eex-use can only be utilised when applying
eu.
