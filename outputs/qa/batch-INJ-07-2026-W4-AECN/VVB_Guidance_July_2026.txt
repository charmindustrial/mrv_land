Scale Tickets — Tractor/Trailer Nomenclature Mismatch (Third-Party Drop-Trailer Offloads)
For certain offloads in this Reporting Period, the tractor and trailer identifiers recorded on the full scale ticket do not match those recorded on the empty scale ticket. This is an operational artifact of a drop-trailer delivery arrangement and does not indicate a mismatched load or an erroneous weighing.
For these deliveries, Charm used a third-party carrier that delivered the loaded trailer to the site and dropped it for offloading. After the bio-oil was offloaded, Charm moved the same trailer with a Charm tractor to the scale to capture the empty (tare) weight. Because the loaded weighing was performed with the third-party tractor attached and the empty (tare) weighing was performed with a Charm tractor, the tractor nomenclature differs between the two scale tickets. The make and model of the trailer — the unit that physically holds and transports the bio-oil — is the same across both the full and empty weighings, and is the basis on which the two tickets are matched for that offload.

Return of Empty IBC Totes to Colorado for Reuse
In this Reporting Period, Charm began returning emptied intermediate bulk container (IBC) totes from the Basco injection site in Louisiana back to its production facility in Fort Lupton, Colorado. This is one of the first periods in which empty totes have been shipped back to Colorado.
As injection throughput has grown, the Louisiana site has accumulated a larger supply of injected (emptied) totes than it needs to keep on hand. Rather than procure new totes, Charm is returning these emptied units to the Colorado production facility so they can be refilled and reused. These return trips carry only empty totes — no bio-oil is transported on the Louisiana-to-Colorado leg.
The transport emissions of these empty-tote return trips are quantified and included in this Reporting Period as one-off logistics movements in the Additional Transportation component in Certify. 
Out-of-Boundary Diversion of Bio-Oil for R&D Testing
During the June 2026 Reporting Period, Charm filled one tote with AECN bio-oil and transported it to Colorado for further research-and-development (R&D) testing. This material lies outside the project boundary: it was not injected for geologic sequestration and is not associated with any removal claim in this or any period. Because the diversion is entirely out of boundary, the emissions from transporting the tote to Colorado are likewise excluded and are not counted against the project.
The diverted mass — 1,142 kg — was debited from batch INJ-06-2026-W2-AECN by reducing the railcar’s closing offload. The reduction is recorded as an Uncertainty Adjustment on Rail_GPRX5106_5.13.2026_offload_4 in the AECN Rail detail tab of the Week 2 ops notes — the same line Charm uses for any downward mass reconciliation applied to a railcar’s fourth (closing) offload.
Although the oil is excluded from the removal claim, Charm has conservatively retained the pyrolysis-stage emissions attributable to producing this bio-oil in the project’s emissions total. The project therefore bears the production emissions for 1,142 kg of bio-oil while claiming no corresponding removal — a treatment that can only understate net sequestration, never inflate it.

Diesel Delivery Transport vs. Diesel Site Emissions
This Reporting Period reports two separate diesel-related emission items that measure different things and should not be conflated. Diesel site emissions capture the combustion emissions of all diesel fuel consumed at the injection site, regardless of how that fuel reached the site. Diesel delivery emissions capture only the transport of getting diesel to the site.
The two differ in scope this period because the diesel was obtained in two ways. One diesel resupply was delivered to the site by a third-party carrier, and that trip is reported under diesel delivery transport. The remaining diesel was collected using Charm's own site work truck; the emissions of those pickup trips are already included in the site's vehicle (gasoline) emissions, so applying a separate diesel-delivery transport charge to that fuel would double-count it. The diesel-delivery transport line therefore reflects only the single delivered resupply, and is independent of the total diesel usage reported under diesel site emissions.
Production Bio-Oil Carbon Content — In-House CHN Analysis (Emissions Allocation Only)
Beginning with the April 2026 Reporting Period, Charm returned to quantifying the carbon content of its production bio-oil using its own in-house CHN (carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen) elemental analysis. This carbon content is used solely to allocate Charm's production-stage emissions across the co-products of pyrolysis; it is not used in the quantification of carbon removals, which continue to rely on independent third-party carbon-content analysis. Charm expects in-house CHN to be the standard source of production carbon content in this and subsequent Reporting Periods.

Trailer Standard Empty Weight (AECN Rail Offloads)
Purpose. For AECN rail offloads, net bio-oil mass injected is the Full scale weight minus the Empty scale weight of the same tractor + trailer. When an offload’s empty scale ticket is missing, or its trailer/tractor does not match the Full ticket (a rig mismatch), the recorded empty weight cannot be used directly. In these cases a conservative per-trailer standard empty weight is substituted.
Method. For each trailer, empty (tare) weights were compiled from all available CAT Scale empty tickets across the April–July 2026 rail offloads (n = 48 valid weighings). The conservative standard empty weight is defined as the per-trailer mean plus one standard deviation. Because a heavier empty weight yields a lower net injected mass, mean + 1σ ensures the substitution is conservative (under-crediting) — consistent with the treatment of WODO standard mass loss.

Excludes non-standard tickets (two third-party “Honorable Transport” weighings and tickets with mis-entered tractor/trailer fields). KL510 is based on n = 4 and will be refined as additional tickets accrue.
Trigger and application. For a given offload, substitute the standard empty weight for the trailer recorded on the Full ticket when either (a) the empty scale ticket is missing, or (b) the empty ticket’s tractor and/or trailer do not match the Full ticket. Substituted values are annotated in the ops-notes Empty Scale Ticket cell.
