Trailer Standard Empty Weight (AECN Rail Offloads)
Purpose. For AECN rail offloads, net bio-oil mass injected is the Full scale weight minus the Empty scale weight of the same tractor + trailer. When an offload’s empty scale ticket is missing, or its trailer/tractor does not match the Full ticket (a rig mismatch), the recorded empty weight cannot be used directly. In these cases a conservative per-trailer standard empty weight is substituted.
Method. Unchanged from the July 1 – July 31 RP. For each trailer, empty (tare) weights were compiled from all available CAT Scale empty tickets across the April–July 2026 rail offloads (n = 48 valid weighings). The conservative standard empty weight is defined as the per-trailer mean plus one standard deviation. Because a heavier empty weight yields a lower net injected mass, mean + 1σ ensures the substitution is conservative (under-crediting) — consistent with the treatment of WODO standard mass loss.
Standard empty weights applied this RP — carried forward unchanged from the July 2026 table:
Trailer        n        Mean (lb)        σ (lb)        Standard empty = mean + 1σ (lb)
KL192        22        29,321        189        29,510
KL188        14        28,997        309        29,307
KL720        8        29,515        130        29,645
KL510        4        28,985        493        29,478
Excludes non-standard tickets (two third-party “Honorable Transport” weighings and tickets with mis-entered tractor/trailer fields). KL510 is based on n = 4 and will be refined as additional tickets accrue. The standards were deliberately not recomputed with August 2026 tickets for this RP, so that the figures applied here are the same published values the July 2026 RP was verified against.
Trailer identification on the tickets. Trailer 192 appears on CAT Scale tickets in several spellings — “KL192”, “RL192” and “KL 192”. These are one physical trailer recorded with data-entry variation in the leading letter and spacing, not separate units. Two observations support this: the spellings never appear on the same date across the 23 weighing dates in the April–August ticket set, whereas Charm runs two rigs concurrently throughout and genuinely distinct trailers co-occur constantly; and the tare distributions are statistically indistinguishable (n = 21, mean 29,118 lb, σ 192 lb for the “KL” spelling against n = 7, mean 29,257 lb, σ 74 lb for the “RL” spelling, the latter range falling entirely inside the former). A full and empty weigh that differ only in this spelling, on the same tractor, is therefore the same rig and is not treated as a mismatch. Two offloads in this Reporting Period fall into that category — GPRX5240 offload 2 in Week 1 and SHPX215090 offload 2 in Week 2 — and both use their as-ticketed pair unchanged. The single published standard for trailer 192 applies whenever a substitution is required for it.
Trigger and application. For a given offload, substitute the standard empty weight for the trailer recorded on the Full ticket when either (a) the empty scale ticket is missing, or (b) the empty ticket’s tractor and/or trailer do not match the Full ticket. Substituted values are annotated in the ops-notes Empty Scale Ticket cell.

QOWV Tote Transport — Totes Routed via Kansas (Weeks 3 and 4)
Purpose. QOWV is Charm’s aqueous bio-oil fraction, shipped from the Colorado production site to the Basco 6 injection site in Louisiana in intermediate bulk containers (IBC totes). Four totes injected in Week 3 (INJ-08-2026-W3) and one injected in Week 4 did not travel that route directly: they were trucked from Colorado to Charm’s Kansas site on 2025-10-21, stored there, and later repositioned from Kansas to Louisiana. Their transport emissions are calculated over the route actually travelled rather than over the standard Colorado to Louisiana route.
Method. Truck transport is calculated as two terms. Vehicle embodied emissions are expressed per mile driven and are independent of load, so the miles of any given truck are apportioned between the batches sharing it, by each batch’s share of the totes on board. Fuel use is expressed per tonne-mile and therefore needs no apportionment: each batch is charged its own cargo mass over the distance that cargo travelled. For the Kansas-routed totes this means an apportioned share of two separate trucks on the embodied term, and their own mass over 1,221 miles on the fuel-use term.
Distances. Charm Colorado to Charm Kansas is 537 miles one way, and Charm Kansas to Basco 6 is 684 miles one way; each is evidenced by a filed route map. The via-Kansas route totals 1,221 miles against 1,201 miles for the direct route, 1.7 per cent longer, so charging the route actually travelled both reflects the physical movement and is the conservative treatment.
Application — Week 3 (four totes, tags 118811, 118837, 118872, 118886):
Leg        Trucks        Totes aboard        Batch totes        Share        Miles        Allocated miles
Colorado to Kansas (shipment #32/#33)        2        34        4        4/34        537 × 2        126.4
Kansas to Basco 6 (repositioning)        1        6        4        4/6        684        456.0
Total allocated miles for the week therefore rise from 1,693.3, covering the 33 totes that travelled the direct route, to 2,275.7.
Which of the two trucks on the Colorado to Kansas shipment carried which tote was not recorded. This does not affect the result, because four totes out of 34 across two trucks allocates the same mileage as four out of 17 on one truck. On the Kansas to Louisiana leg, six totes are used as the denominator, being the totes that returned from Kansas; a larger denominator would allocate fewer miles, so six is the conservative reading.
Application — Week 4 (one tote, tag 118846). The same two legs apply, allocating 31.6 miles and 114.0 miles respectively, 145.6 miles in total.
Carbon intensity is unaffected. Pyrolysis emissions for these totes remain keyed to the month in which the oil was produced, not to the route taken or to the week of injection.
QOWV Tote Transport — Vehicle Class Emission Factors
Purpose. The emission factors applied to QOWV tote transport were corrected during this reporting period, so the factors used here differ from those applied in the July 1 – July 31 RP.
Correction. QOWV totes travel on a flatbed trailer, not in a tanker. Until this period the calculation applied tanker emission factors to both QOWV transport terms. Both now use the flatbed and non-tanker factors, which are the factors the equivalent WODO tote transport has always used:
• Fuel use, well to wheel — 0.1143 replaced by 0.1384 kg CO₂e per tonne-mile (flatbed truck).
• Vehicle embodied — 0.23756 replaced by 0.19279 kg CO₂e per mile (non-tanker truck).
Direction and effect. The correction raises the mass-dependent fuel-use term and lowers the per-mile embodied term. On balance it increases reported QOWV transport emissions and so reduces the net removal, by approximately 0.9 tonnes CO₂e for Week 3. The emission factors for AECN bio-oil, which does travel by tanker, are unchanged.


AECN Pyrolysis Process Emissions — Updated H2 2026 Emission Factor
Purpose. Pyrolysis process emissions for bio-oil produced by Bioénergie AE Côte-Nord (AECN) are calculated from a carbon intensity supplied by AECN's own life-cycle assessment. AECN reissues that assessment each half-year. The assessment built on January to June 2026 operating data became available during this reporting period and supersedes the one applied through the July 1 – July 31 RP. It governs every AECN removal in this period and in subsequent H2 2026 periods.
Method. The process carbon intensity is the sum of two terms. Stack emissions are calculated from the GHGenius Ensyn RTP model: methane and nitrous oxide intensities of 0.34 and 1.01 grams per gigajoule of feedstock are converted to a bio-oil mass basis using the plant's biomass throughput, bio-oil yield, higher heating value and bio-oil density for the period, then weighted with the IPCC AR6 100-year global warming potentials of 27 for non-fossil methane and 273 for nitrous oxide. All remaining process emissions are taken directly from the vendor's life-cycle assessment. The two terms are summed without further adjustment.
Term        Value (t CO₂e per tonne of bio-oil)
Stack emissions (GHGenius Ensyn RTP model)        0.0040651
Other process emissions (vendor life-cycle assessment)        0.1330520
Total process carbon intensity        0.1371171
Values applied. The total, 0.1371171 t CO₂e per tonne of bio-oil, applies to every AECN removal in this period, irrespective of how the bio-oil reached the injection site. Pyrolysis process emissions arise at the AECN production facility and are complete before the oil is loaded for shipment, so the process carbon intensity cannot depend on whether a batch subsequently travelled by rail or by direct truck. Delivery mode is accounted for separately, in the transport components. A carbon intensity expressed in tonnes CO₂e per tonne is numerically identical to one expressed in kilograms CO₂e per kilogram, which is why the same figure appears against both unit labels in the calculation.
Direction and effect. The revised factor is higher than the factor previously applied, so it increases reported pyrolysis process emissions and reduces net removals. It is therefore the conservative treatment. The effect on this reporting period is as follows.
Removal        Pyrolysis mass (kg)        Additional process emissions (kg CO₂e)
Week 1 (rail)        213,775        7,080
Week 2 (rail)        213,352        7,066
Week 3 (rail)        424,109        14,045
Week 4 (rail)        77,718        2,574
Week 4 (direct truck)        78,880        795
Total                31,559
Net removals across the period fall by 31.6 t CO₂e, between 2.6 and 2.7 per cent of each rail removal and 0.8 per cent of the direct-truck removal.
Scope. The revision affects the process term only. Pyrolysis embodied emissions are a separate factor, derived from a separate calculation, and are unchanged at 0.02116 t CO₂e per tonne of bio-oil. The updated factor has been applied to all AECN removals in this reporting period, including those quantified earlier in the period before the assessment was issued, so that a single process carbon intensity governs the whole period rather than changing partway through it.
Evidence. Three documents support the factor: the stack-emissions calculation, the vendor's life-cycle carbon intensity calculation, and the vendor's supporting greenhouse gas data as at 30 June 2026. All three are attached to the pyrolysis process carbon intensity of every AECN removal in this period. The corresponding documents from the superseded assessment have been detached, so that the evidence attached to each removal is only the assessment that produces the value applied to it.
Kerry Calciner Oil — Correction to the Solids-Content Rationale
Purpose. The July 1 – July 31 RP guidance stated that Kerry calciner oil “measures materially higher in solids than AECN oil”, and argued from that premise that applying the AECN value to a Kerry load would overstate the mass credited as injected. The measured constants do not support either statement. The Kerry treatment applied in this Reporting Period is unchanged and remains correct; only the stated rationale is corrected here.
The measured constants. Both values are applied as they appear on the constants block of the relevant tab of the weekly ops notes.
Feedstock        Solids content applied        Source
Kerry calciner oil        0.071% (0.00071)        CAR-474, SGS, Manitowoc calciner phase, measured 2026-05-28
AECN oil        0.195% (0.00195)        AECN-derived value applied to Charm’s other third-party oil
Correction. Kerry calciner oil measures materially LOWER in solids than AECN oil, not higher — the AECN figure is 2.75 times the Kerry figure. It follows that using Kerry’s own measured value subtracts less filtered mass, and therefore credits slightly more mass as injected, than applying the AECN value would. The direction of the July statement, and the conservatism argument built on it, were both inverted.
Effect. On the single Kerry tanker load in this Reporting Period (INJ-08-2026-W3, net oil 10,949.71 kg) the two constants differ as follows.
Constant applied        Filtration loss (kg)        Mass injected (kg)
Kerry 0.071% (applied)        7.77        10,941.94
AECN 0.195% (not applied)        21.35        10,928.36
The difference is 13.58 kg of injected mass, equivalent to 26.8 kg CO₂e, or 0.127 per cent of that removal’s net. It is immaterial to the quantification.
Why Kerry’s own value remains the correct one. Solids loading is a property of the specific feedstock rather than of the filter, and Kerry calciner oil has been directly measured. A feedstock-specific measured value is preferred to a value derived from a different feedstock whether the measured figure happens to be higher or lower than that alternative. Charm does not select between the two constants on the basis of which yields the smaller credit; it applies the measurement belonging to the oil in question. The 25 µm wellhead filter and the loss treatment are unchanged — Kerry takes a filtration deduction only, and no sparging loss.
Open item. The Slack announcement of CAR-474 quotes the Kerry figure as 0.71 per cent, ten times the 0.071 per cent recorded in the CAR-474 data file. Three copies of the data file agree on 0.071 per cent, so the file is treated as authoritative and the announcement as a transcription error. A written confirmation from the analyst has been requested. The applied constant will not be changed before that confirmation is received.
