Trailer Standard Empty Weight (AECN Rail Offloads)

Purpose. For AECN rail offloads, net bio-oil mass injected is the Full scale weight minus the Empty scale weight of the same tractor + trailer. When an offload’s empty scale ticket is missing, or its trailer/tractor does not match the Full ticket (a rig mismatch), the recorded empty weight cannot be used directly. In these cases a conservative per-trailer standard empty weight is substituted.

Method. For each trailer, empty (tare) weights were compiled from all available CAT Scale empty tickets across the April–July 2026 rail offloads (n = 48 valid weighings). The conservative standard empty weight is defined as the per-trailer mean plus one standard deviation. Because a heavier empty weight yields a lower net injected mass, mean + 1σ ensures the substitution is conservative (under-crediting) — consistent with the treatment of WODO standard mass loss.

Standard empty weights (as of July 2026):

Trailer        n        Mean (lb)        σ (lb)        Standard empty = mean + 1σ (lb)

KL192        22        29,321        189        29,510

KL188        14        28,997        309        29,307

KL720        8        29,515        130        29,645

KL510        4        28,985        493        29,478

Excludes non-standard tickets (two third-party “Honorable Transport” weighings and tickets with mis-entered tractor/trailer fields). KL510 is based on n = 4 and will be refined as additional tickets accrue.

Trigger and application. For a given offload, substitute the standard empty weight for the trailer recorded on the Full ticket when either (a) the empty scale ticket is missing, or (b) the empty ticket’s tractor and/or trailer do not match the Full ticket. Substituted values are annotated in the ops-notes Empty Scale Ticket cell.

Kerry Calciner Oil — Bulk Tanker Delivery and Kerry-Specific Solids Content

Delivery form. In this Reporting Period, Kerry calciner oil is delivered to the Basco injection site in bulk tanker trailers rather than in IBC totes as in prior Periods. Delivered mass is therefore established from CAT Scale tickets — the full weight less the post-offload empty (tare) weight of the same tractor and trailer — rather than from per-tote tag weights. There is no per-tote record for a tanker load; the scale tickets are the primary mass evidence.

Filtered mass — Kerry-specific solids content. Kerry oil passes through the same 25 µm wellhead filter as Charm’s other injected oil, and the solids retained by that filter are subtracted from the delivered mass to arrive at the mass of bio-oil injected. For Kerry, that subtraction uses the measured solids content of Kerry calciner oil itself, not the AECN-derived solids content that Charm applies to its other third-party oil. Testing is in the Additional Documentation folder.

Solids loading is a property of the specific feedstock, not of the filter. Kerry calciner oil measures materially higher in solids than AECN oil, so applying the AECN value to a Kerry load would understate the filtered mass and correspondingly overstate the mass credited as injected. Using Kerry’s own measured solids content is both the feedstock-accurate and the conservative treatment. The Kerry solids value and its source analysis are cited on the constants block of the Kerry tab in the weekly ops notes; the AECN value remains in use, unchanged, for all other third-party oil.

Loss treatment. Kerry takes a filtration deduction only. No sparging loss is applied to Kerry oil (because Kerry is not sparged), and the deduction appears in the Filtration column — not the Sparging column — of the weekly ops-notes summary.

pH tracking. The oil that is unloaded from the truck is held in a dedicated tank and progressively injected in combination with AECN injections as the high viscosity of the oil precludes independent injection. While the totes were blended with sparged AECN, trucked Kerry oil is progressively co-injected with AECN over the course of the month and the associated removal is not considered complete until the tank is empty. However, this means that we do not have an independent pH value that is specific to Kerry. Rather, the pH measured over the course of the month’s AECN injections is net of Kerry oil as well.

Standard H1 2026 CI for Charm bio-oil

Beginning this Reporting Period, Charm is harmonizing our bio-oil CI update cadence with AECN and updating our production CI for pyrolysis products every six months. As with AECN, the data from the prior six months of production is used to compute the CI of each product in the same way it is computed in monthly Production Periods. For the subsequent six months, those CIs are used to compute production emissions netted against removals for all Charm bio-oil.

 In this case, the data from H1 of 2026 sets the CI which is in turn used for removals injected through 12/31. Beginning 1/1 through 6/30 of 2027, a new CI using the production data from H2 of 2026 and so on.

For this Reporting Period, the Charm BO MRV folder contains production data from the 6 monthly periods in the first half of 2026 and a sheet showing how the outputs of each production month are summed and divided to compute a 6-month CI.

Note: For March Output, 6 WODO results were returned instead of the usual 5. The highest value was dropped as an outlier (60.6% vs. the next highest result of 56.5%). These results are used for allocation only, so do not impact gross removal magnitude.

Additional Transport Empty Rail Process Emissions

Mass for the ton-mile calculation for the rail journeys covered by the rail “additional transport” Site Emissions was set at 1,000kg. The cars were shipped unloaded, but setting the mass at 0 kg zeroes out the emission, which is clearly incorrect. So, 1,000kg is used as a proxy value to ensure a reasonable estimate of emissions from moving the railcars with no cargo.
