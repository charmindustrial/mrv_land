AECN Rail — Oil Withdrawn for Research and Development (Week 3, INJ-09-2026-W3)
Purpose. On 12 September 2026, within Week 3 of this Reporting Period (INJ-09-2026-W3, 11 – 18 September 2026), two hundred US gallons of AECN third-party bio-oil were drawn from the week’s aggregate AECN oil, placed in a tote, and shipped to Charm’s Colorado facility for equipment testing of a new grinding pump. This oil was not injected during this Reporting Period, so it is excluded from the Mass of Bio-Oil Injected (MOBI) claimed for the week.
Method. The withdrawn volume is converted to mass using the AECN bio-oil density measured by SGS on the Week 3 composite sample (1.192 kg/L), and the result is subtracted from the week’s AECN rail MOBI:
Mass removed for R&D (kg) = 200 gal × 3.785411784 L/gal × 1.192 kg/L = 902.44 kg
Values applied. AECN rail MOBI for Week 3 is 194,394.38 kg before the deduction and 193,491.94 kg after it. The latter is the Mass of Injectant reported on the Week 3 AECN removal. The deduction is shown in Section 4 (Rollup) of the AECN Rail tab of the Week 3 ops notes, in the column “Mass Removed for R&D (kg)”, with the density, gallon count and conversion factor held as labelled constants on the same tab.
Direction and effect. Conservative. The withdrawn mass is removed from the credited injected mass in full, while rail and tanker transport emissions, sparging loss and filtration loss continue to be calculated on the full delivered and offloaded mass of the railcars, including the oil that was later withdrawn. Emissions are therefore not reduced to match the smaller credited mass.
Scope. Week 3 only. No other week in this Reporting Period includes a withdrawal of oil for research and development. Should this oil be returned to the injection site and injected in a later Reporting Period, it will be documented and claimed in that period only, against its own records, and will not be counted twice.
AECN Rail — New Rail Destination at Baton Rouge (Railcar SHPX215053, Week 4, INJ-09-2026-W4)
The change. Beginning in late September 2026, AECN railcars are routed to a transload rail spur in Baton Rouge, Louisiana, instead of the rail yard at Opelousas, Louisiana. At Baton Rouge the oil is transferred from the railcar to tanker trucks and hauled by road to the injection site. The first transload at Baton Rouge took place on 22 September 2026.
Scope. In this Reporting Period, one railcar used the new route: SHPX215053, offloaded in Week 4 (INJ-09-2026-W4, 18 – 25 September 2026). Every other railcar in this Reporting Period was received at Opelousas under the existing routing. From the next Reporting Period onward, Baton Rouge is the destination for all AECN rail deliveries and will be treated as the standard route.
Method. Because the rail distance and the tanker distance for SHPX215053 differ from those of the railcars received at Opelousas, the rail transport and the tanker transport to the injection site for this railcar are calculated and reported separately from the other Week 4 railcars rather than pooled with them. Each leg uses this railcar’s own mass and its own route distance, so the Opelousas distances are not applied to SHPX215053 and the Baton Rouge distances are not applied to any other railcar. The tanker leg is counted as a round trip, consistent with the treatment of the Opelousas leg.
Values applied. Rail distance from the loading origin to Baton Rouge: 2,152 (map evidence is added in drive). Tanker distance from Baton Rouge to the injection site: 136 mi one way, doubled for the round trip. Mass basis: the same scale-ticket net masses and loading Bill of Lading masses used for every other railcar.
Evidence. Week 4 ops notes, AECN Rail tab (Section 1 for the railcar’s route distances, Section 3 for the four offloads); certified truck scale tickets for each offload; loading Bills of Lading.
AECN Rail — Bio-oil Loss
Purpose. For each AECN railcar, the mass loaded at the origin (the sum of the railcar’s loading Bills of Lading) can exceed the mass delivered to the injection site (the sum of the certified truck scale tickets across the railcar’s four offloads). The difference is bio-oil that Charm received and transported but did not inject. Its upstream emissions, pyrolysis and transport from the loading origin to the rail yard, remain attributable to Charm and are reported on each AECN rail removal as a separate component, AECN Bio-oil Loss.
Method. Pyrolysis and all transport components on the removal are calculated on the delivered mass: scale-ticket net mass, less any Uncertainty Adjustment. The lost mass, loaded mass minus delivered mass, is quantified per railcar and recognised in the week in which the railcar’s final offload is completed; a railcar that is still being offloaded carries no loss until it closes. Where a railcar’s scale tickets exceed its Bills of Lading, the excess is removed from the final offload through the Uncertainty Adjustment and the railcar’s loss is zero. Loss emissions apply the same AECN pyrolysis carbon intensities (process and embodied) and the same loading-origin-to-rail-yard transport emissions used for the delivered oil, pro-rated to the lost mass, which equates to approximately 0.229 t CO₂e per tonne of oil lost. Delivered oil and lost oil together therefore carry the pyrolysis and upstream transport emissions of the full loaded mass. (Find the loss column in the AECN Rail tab on Column G)
Evidence. The loss emission figure is calculated in Charm’s dMRV platform, Mangrove Systems. A platform-generated calculation record cannot yet be attached to each specific Certify datapoint; until it can, we have an example calculation attached to show the method. Charm and Mangrove are currently working on getting this evidence available so that it is tailored to each specific loss component. If any specific clarification or calculations are requested, Charm and Mangrove can provide these upon request, although the methodology and EFs will not change, only the mass variable.
Evidence. Historically, Charm had allocated these emissions in the pyrolysis and transport emissions. This change will keep these separated for accuracy and tracking purposes. 
Site Gasoline — E-85 Fills Quantified at an E-85 Emission Factor 
Purpose. Site vehicles at the injection site are fuelled on a Charm fleet fuel card. In this Reporting Period, five of the seven fills were dispensed as E-85, an ethanol–gasoline blend, and two as conventional unleaded gasoline. Charm will be using an emission factor specific to E-85 this reporting period.
Method. The E-85 factor is a well-to-wheel factor derived from Argonne National Laboratory’s R&D GREET 2025 Revision 1 model, the same source used for ethanol, methanol, and several material factors in Charm’s Standard Emission Factor list. GREET publishes a blended well-to-wheel result for E-85 in a flexible-fuel vehicle (corn ethanol, 85 per cent ethanol by volume) and a separate result for conventional gasoline. The well-to-wheel intensity of the ethanol component is backed out of the blended E-85 result on an energy-share basis, and the fuel is then re-blended at the ethanol content assumed for this Reporting Period: heating value and density are volume-weighted from the GREET fuel specifications, and well-to-wheel emissions are allocated by energy share. The derivation is held on its own tab of the Standard Emission Factor list, with every input traceable to a cell in the GREET workbook.
Blend basis. Retail E-85 is not a fixed blend. ASTM D5798 permits an ethanol content of between 51 and 83 per cent by volume, adjusted by season and region, and no blend certificate is held for these fills. The factor is therefore calculated at the minimum permitted ethanol content of 51 per cent (E51). Because ethanol carries a lower well-to-wheel intensity than gasoline under this accounting boundary, assuming the least ethanol the standard allows produces the highest emission factor within the standard.
Values applied.
Parameter        Value
E-85 well-to-wheel emission factor, E51 basis        2.4701 kg CO₂e per kg fuel
E-85 well-to-wheel emission factor, E51 basis        7.1961 kg CO₂e per US gallon
E-85 density, E51 basis        2.9133 kg per US gallon
Conventional gasoline (unchanged)        3.74 kg CO₂e per kg fuel (GLEC Framework v3.2)




Site Diesel — Rail-Yard Air Compressor Fuel
Purpose. AECN railcars are offloaded into tanker trucks at the rail yard with a towable diesel air compressor (Airman PDS185S, 185 cfm). For operational efficiency, Charm is starting to use the diesel from the trucks used to offload the oil from the railyard to fill the compressor. This diesel is quantified by receipts or transport process emissions. Since neither of these methods will capture the exact diesel used in the compressor without double counting, Charm is using a conservative approach to estimate the amount of diesel used by the compressor at the railyard. 
Method. Compressor diesel is estimated from the number of rail offloads in the Reporting Period, the compressor run time per offload, and the manufacturer’s full-load fuel consumption, and is converted to emissions with the same diesel density and emission factor applied to all other site diesel:
Diesel consumed (US gal) = rail offloads × 2 h per offload × 2.4 US gal per h
Values applied.
Parameter        Value
Rail offloads (Weeks 1 – 4; ten railcars, four offloads each)        40
Compressor run time per offload (site management estimate, 4 cars are offloaded per day, so this is a reasonable estimate given a standard 8hr day)        2 h
Fuel consumption at full load (manufacturer specification)        2.4 US gal per h
Diesel consumed        192 US gal
Diesel density        3.22 kg per US gal
Diesel emission factor (GLEC Framework v3.2)        3.87 kg CO₂e per kg fuel
Emissions        2,392.59 kg CO₂e
Direction and effect. Adds 2,392.59 kg CO₂e to reported site diesel emissions for this Reporting Period. The estimate is conservative: the full-load fuel rate is applied to all run time, although the compressor spends part of each offload at partial load or idle, where the manufacturer rates consumption at 1.7 US gal per h (70 per cent load) and 0.8 US gal per h (no load). There is no double count, because no tractor fuel purchase enters any emission calculation in this Reporting Period.

Immateriality of Argon Transport and removal from calculations
Transporting argon to the Louisiana injection site for the use as an inert gas during welding has historically been quantified as its own set of process and embodied emissions. As operations have become more predictable, Charm has determined that the quantification of these emissions to be immaterial to the reporting period until the use of more argon on the site is required. Argon will continue to be quantified. 
Direction and effect. The total emissions of argon transport in September was less than 10 kgs. Process 0.88kg and EE 9 kg.
