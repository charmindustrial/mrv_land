Hi Tim,
Thanks for the feedback, responses are below.
1. WODO loss factor
    a. All good — This was weighed on one of the same CO scales that I sent calibration over for in October 2025. I'll provide updated certificates at the year mark as usual.
2. 6-month CI
    a. I understand the concern for sure. The goal here is not to create significant reporting discrepancies, but to make both verification and reporting more efficient without doing that
        i. Structurally, the 6-month refresh acts as a backstop here. Any changes in production, emissions, etc. are included in the data built up over a given 6-month period. This ensures that any loss or gain of efficiency is reflected in the subsequent period
        ii. In addition, extending the period helps normalize big swings, positive or negative, that occur over time (e.g. seasonal climate impacting production). This ensures that each kg of oil is more representative of the production process as a whole rather than having a series of disparate CIs following relatively small volumes of oil
3. 6-month Monitoring
    a. That's fine — Reducing frequency for LA data has the least speed/efficiency gain as well, so we can continue with that as usual
4. Amortization
    a. The amortization unit will be kg CO2e/tCDR (gross). That's meant to align with GHG Accounting Section 7, option 3, amortizing "Per tonne of CO2 sequestered, based on estimated total gross CO2 sequestration over the Project lifetime.
    b. Yes, that's understood. Part of the logic here is that two things are true at once: 1) We are running on the best forecast we have available that are literally used to plan the business, and 2) They are often inaccurate as even the best projections tend to be, particularly when deploying new technologies and processes. This system builds in the corrective mechanism explicitly, rather than assuming a given set of projections are correct and then having to start from scratch if they aren't.
